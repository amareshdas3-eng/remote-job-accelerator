## 4. Key Interview Talking Points & High-Impact Responses

### Q1: "How do you manage the risk of hallucinations in production AI workflows?"
> *"In our architecture, we treat LLM generation strictly as an unverified draft proposal, never ground truth. We enforce a two-stage firewall: first, a server-side Policy Guard audits all generated claims against an immutable cryptographic snapshot of verified candidate data, blocking ungrounded claims before review. Second, we enforce a Sovereign Human Review Gate where only an authenticated human can authorize execution. In our production validation, this resulted in a 100.0% claim verification rate with zero escaped hallucinations across all dispatched applications."*

### Q2: "How do you prevent scope creep and maintain architectural integrity across project releases?"
> *"We implement strict phase-gate governance with explicit architectural invariants. In RJA, we maintained a zero-drift constraint on our core execution substrates—any change to `lib/execution/` or the agent authority registry was an automatic gate failure. Furthermore, we formalized the 12-Field RFC standard requiring statistical qualification (Wilson confidence intervals across verified denominators) before approving new capabilities. Finally, upon hitting our milestone targets, we executed a permanent architecture freeze with a defect-only exception policy to prevent speculative feature creep."*

### Q3: "How does your engineering background influence your approach to software and AI projects?"
> *"Classical systems and electrical engineering instill a profound respect for boundary conditions, physical constraints, and fail-closed safety. When building software—particularly with probabilistic components like LLMs—I apply the same rigorous mindset: separate control logic from power delivery, define negative capabilities, enforce cryptographic determinism, and measure every outcome empirically rather than relying on vendor benchmarks."*

### Q4: "How do you balance AI capabilities with commercial unit economics?"
> *"We design for economics from day one. In RJA, we implemented prompt optimization, schema compression, and multi-model pool cascading, keeping mean inference cost to $0.0404 per completed application against a $0.05 limit. Combined with reducing human review time down to ~2 minutes, total operational unit cost is $2.07 against a $10.00+ commercial value, yielding sustainable gross margins exceeding 79%."*
